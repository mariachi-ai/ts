# Show one thing

**The job:** show one entry (or one article) to read, with what it belongs to.

## What Vellum does

1. **A plain bar on top:** Close (`x`) and the full date in subtle text at the left; More and New at the right, 18px grey glyphs, no fill.
2. **The text** at a reading width (at most 40rem) in the entry face (Lato) at 17px, line height 1.65, in the softer ink; the title in the entry face, 24px bold.
3. **Pictures** full width of the text column with 6px corners; quotes in muted text with a hairline at the left.
4. **A meta footer:** the journal's name in accent text, weather and place in subtle text, Tags and Attach glyphs at the right.

On a public help article (`reading`) the same job uses the interface face: a 34px bold title, a 20px bold lede, 17px text, 20px bold section headings, related guides as blue links, and a grey side panel with one action.

## Where it appears

- `list-detail` (the open entry), `reading` (a help article).
- Source: the entry pane is `observed` in the desktop app screenshot (earlier study); the help-article layout (title, bold lede, side panel with one action) is `observed` on the studied help articles (private notes).

## Variations and triggers

- **An entry** → the entry face, no side panel.
- **A help article** → the interface face, a breadcrumb, search above, the side panel.

## What it does not do

- No cards around the text; no sidebar of metadata beside an entry.
- No text wider than 40rem.
- No coloured headings.

## Why

- `observed`: the source's editor sets entry text in a humanist face a size above the interface.
- `inferred`: the writing is the page; chrome stays thin and grey.
